# Execution Contract

Use this before edits, process execution, challenge/retraction handling, or final delivery.

## During Execution

Execution may resolve routine mechanics, but it does not erase the ratified boundary. A fork
exists when a reasonable user might choose differently and the choice changes what ships.
Mechanical placement, a reversible label, or illustrative phrasing is not a fork unless
downstream work will inherit it as fact.

When new evidence invalidates a premise or changes scope, ownership, effect, or deliverable
shape, return to one-question mode for that fork. Lead with the evidence and recommendation,
record the decision, and recap again before hard-to-reverse work resumes.

A reversible additive choice that contradicts no ratified decision may proceed after immediate
ledger capture. A form change may do the same only when it preserves substance; disclose it in
the recap. Adjacent defects remain out of scope unless their repair is mechanically required
to deliver the ratified work and is cheap, additive, and reversible; otherwise ask or defer.

Follow [Questions And Ledger](questions.md#running-ledger): initialize a ledger before
multi-file or multi-phase mutation, capture every qualifying fork before the next mutation,
and sweep it into the final summary.

## Sub-Processes And Gates

When an invoked sub-process owns a user-facing approval gate, give it the ratified inputs and
let that gate serve as the single checkpoint. Do not add a duplicate confirmation or suppress
the sub-process's gate.

When the current thread executes the sub-process directly, present that process's own recap in
the current thread. If a named workflow is unavailable but its repository contract is
readable, disclose the fallback, follow the contract, and preserve its gates. Without a
readable contract or sufficient authority, stop at the narrowest safe handoff.

Before product-code edits, check for and use the repository's implementation workflow when it
exists. If it is intentionally inapplicable, record why before editing.

## Retractions And Challenges

If the user retracts a ratified item, keep unaffected items ratified and rework only that item
and its dependents. If the user challenges the result's accuracy or completeness, re-check the
challenged claim against the ratified scope, correct the artifact and framing, and disclose
the correction. Do not restart the whole interview unless the rationale invalidates the rest.

## Final Reconciliation

Before delivery:

1. Restate what the recap put in and out of scope.
2. Reconcile the actual diff or deliverable with that boundary.
3. Sweep mid-execution forks from the ledger into the summary.
4. Scope words such as complete, full, or done to the ratified deliverable.
5. Run the repository's completion/landing contract. For a structured landing, read it before
   editing as well as at closeout.

## Artifact-Specific Closeout

For a non-product durable artifact such as a skill, template, or process document, name the
artifact class and why it was in scope, include a literal provenance line naming the evidence
that justified it, state the convention checked, and report any applicable mirror, pointer,
formatter, validator, or equivalent proof.

When a companion domain workflow was used, include a domain-doc outcome line for every
glossary or ADR surface it requires, naming the update or saying none was needed and citing
the evidence for that disposition.
